Many learning problems require representing relations among groups of objects rather than features of isolated objects. Relational convolutional networks are designed for that setting: they learn hierarchical relational representations through a composition of modules that detect relational patterns.

## Relational Graphlet Filters

The key operation is a relational convolution. Instead of sliding a spatial filter over neighboring pixels, the model convolves learnable graphlet filters against subsets of objects. A graphlet filter acts as a template for a relational pattern among a group of objects.

Composing these operations yields a hierarchy:

- lower layers detect simpler relational patterns,
- higher layers compose those patterns into more complex higher-order relations.

This gives the architecture an inductive bias for relational structure without requiring a fully symbolic representation.

## Why Hierarchy Matters

Relational tasks often have nested structure. A model may need to detect relations between objects, relations between groups, or patterns that only emerge after composing lower-level relations. The paper studies how a neural architecture can make that hierarchy explicit.

Experiments include relational benchmarks where hierarchical structure is central, including relational games and set-like tasks.

## Connection To The Research Hub

This work is an early piece in a broader line of architectural approaches to relational reasoning. [Abstractors](/writing/research/abstractor) use relational cross-attention, while [Dual Attention Transformers](/writing/research/dual-attention) integrate sensory and relational attention inside a Transformer block. Relational convolutions approach the problem through learnable graphlet-style filters and composition.

## Takeaway

Relational convolutions provide a neural primitive for learning relational features over groups of objects. The architectural bias is useful when the relevant representation is not just object-level features, but higher-order structure among those objects.
